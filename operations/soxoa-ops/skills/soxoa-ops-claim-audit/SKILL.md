# Audit positioning and evidence

Use the supplied copy or pages the user authorizes. Include titles, descriptions, social previews and structured data when in scope; old claims can survive outside visible body copy. Ask for missing product facts or evidence that materially affects the audit, and keep unavailable evidence marked unverified.

Read the opening as a prospective buyer: who it serves, what it does, the problem it addresses and the supported reason to choose it. Distinguish observed copy from your judgment about clarity. Do not pretend a timed glance by the agent is a measured human five-second test. Recommend a focused position without inventing a market-leading niche or customer perception.

Use explicit practical criteria: audience clarity, problem clarity, concrete product mechanism, credible outcome, meaningful differentiation, understandable action and consistency across pages. If the user requests a named marketing framework, use the supplied rubric or verify the relevant source instead of fabricating its rules or scoring an unavailable standard.

Build a material claim ledger with exact wording, page/location, date, evidence, supported scope and recommended keep/reword/remove action. Include metrics, customer counts, testimonials, logos, awards, certifications, compliance language, guarantees and simulated activity indicators. Missing evidence does not prove a claim false; it means the audit cannot verify it. Give special attention to time windows, denominators, units, customer permission and whether examples are labeled.

Replacement wording must stay within supported facts. A future ambition should be labeled as a plan, not current traction. Preserve approved quotations and do not invent a founder quote. Distinguish an observed security or consent check from a legal conclusion or current certification; verify time-sensitive factual claims with primary sources when required and available.

Return the material findings, a concise prioritized fix list, supported replacement copy and what already works. Label assumptions, inaccessible pages and source cutoffs. Audit or drafting alone does not publish or deploy changes; an implementation request may authorize edits under the applicable repository and release procedures.
