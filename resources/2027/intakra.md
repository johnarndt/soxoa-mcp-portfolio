# How to evaluate buying-signal tools for 2027

2027 planning edition. Facts checked October 26, 2026. Recheck product capabilities and terms before purchase.

**Publisher disclosure:** Soxoa operates Soxoa, MentionedOn, Intakra and Portravo. These are practical evaluation criteria from the product team, not an independent vendor ranking.

Evaluate a buying-signal tool on whether a salesperson can verify the event and make a better account decision. Test account fit, evidence quality and timing separately, then measure the review effort. A high score is not proof that a company intends to buy. 2027 planning edition; facts checked Oct 26 2026; update before purchase.

## Write the decision the tool should improve

Choose the action you want to improve: selecting accounts for research, spotting a project that fits your offer, or deciding which existing accounts deserve attention this week. A product that produces more alerts can still create more work without improving that decision.

Define an eligible account before reviewing the signals. Record the industries, operating conditions, company size or geography that matter to what you actually sell. Also record exclusions. A genuine project at an unsuitable company should remain outside the priority list.

- Account fit: could this organization use what we sell?
- Event: what public change actually occurred?
- Relevance: what problem could that change create?
- Timing: what evidence makes attention useful now?

## Inspect the evidence behind a sample alert

Ask to open the original source. Can you identify the company, event date, publication date and reason the event was surfaced? A source that republishes an announcement is not a second independent observation. A similarly named business is not the same account.

Have a reviewer distinguish what the source states from what the tool infers. A job listing may establish that a role is advertised; the conclusion that a new system will be purchased is a hypothesis. The review should preserve that difference in the text a salesperson receives.

- Original source and stable account identity.
- Event time separated from detection time.
- Quoted or summarized fact separated from interpretation.
- Missing or inaccessible evidence shown as a gap.

## Check coverage on your own account list

Create an approved evaluation list from the accounts you could realistically serve. Include the segments that matter to your business, including companies with little public activity. Agree on the evaluation period before the test begins.

Record whether each source can actually be observed for each account. Silence can mean there was no relevant event, the source was unavailable or the tool did not cover it. Ask the vendor how those situations appear. Coverage is especially important when a sales process depends on one source type.

- Use the same account list when comparing tools.
- Include quiet accounts as well as obvious recent events.
- Track coverage gaps independently from irrelevant alerts.

## Test whether the timing claim is justified

Ask what makes the suggested window relevant to your offer. The same leadership change could matter differently to a recruiter, an implementation partner and a software vendor. A generic countdown cannot resolve those differences.

Look for facts that weaken the proposed opportunity: a completed implementation, an incumbent contract, an unrelated job role or a project outside your market. Record these before deciding to investigate. Public evidence can justify research without establishing a confirmed budget or a planned purchase.

- Write one alternative explanation for the event.
- Record what would make the account too early, too late or irrelevant.
- Assign a next fact to verify rather than treating the alert as a finished qualification.

## Measure useful decisions and review burden

For the evaluation period, label each reviewed alert: relevant for investigation, worth watching, outside account fit, wrong company, stale, unsupported or duplicate. Keep a note explaining the label so another reviewer can audit the result.

Report the denominator with every rate. For example, relevant reviewed alerts divided by all reviewed alerts describes your sample's usefulness; it does not measure all opportunities the tool might have missed. Investigate missed known events separately and retain uncertainty where no complete reference set exists.

Measure time spent checking sources and correcting records. Later, track conversations or opportunities separately from alert quality. Avoid attributing revenue to an alert merely because the account eventually bought.

- Useful reviewed decisions / all reviewed alerts.
- Median review time and total reviewer effort.
- Coverage by account segment and source type.
- Known missed events, with sources and detection expectations.

## Match the handoff to the team's actual work

Choose a review destination the team already uses and confirm that the evidence remains attached. Assign a person to decide what happens next. A suggested role still requires verification; a public event does not establish permission to message someone.

Intakra's current homepage describes monitoring public records for tracked companies and delivering dated proof, an interpretation, a relevant role and rough timing. It says it is not a contact database and does not write outbound emails. Evaluate that scope against your process before buying.
